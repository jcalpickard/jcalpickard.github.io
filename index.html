Hello, I’m Justin. I’m an independent researcher and editor, readily nerd-sniped by edge cases and emerging phenomena.

From a home base in the haunted, flood-prone city of York, I combine ethnographic observation and experimental methods to understand how people make sense of the world. Emphasising careful observation and documentation, I move between ground-level details and broader patterns to reveal hidden connections and meanings.

Approach

I work through writing, observation, and making – using each to complicate and enhance the others. My practice unfolds through varied collaborative configurations, from research partnerships to team ethnography, creating spaces where different approaches and insights can cross-pollinate. Rather than fixed protocols, I develop frameworks that flex and evolve with the work at hand, combining multiple forms of sensing and validation.

Based in Yorkshire since 2017, I maintain connections across the north of England’s interconnected cities, particularly through networks of researchers and creative technologists in Manchester and Leeds. This positioning supports an engagement with different rhythms and perspectives, alongside ongoing collaborations across Europe – most recently exploring intersections of ethnography and computing with colleagues in Catalonia.

Core interests

Area Focus
Improvised worlds How people work within and around complex situations through practical action. Tracking informal innovations and creative responses to constraint, particularly in changing urban environments and community contexts.
Knowledge in practice How different groups and communities develop and share practical understanding. Exploring different approaches to collective sense-making and documentation. Particular interest in how technologies mediate cultural transmission.
Experimental methods Combining ethnographic observation with prototyping and emerging computational approaches. Developing new tools for documentation and analysis.

Current work

I’m developing approaches that expand ethnographic practice through tool-making, software development, and fieldwork. This involves crafting prototypes, situated software, and experimental frameworks that serve both as instruments of inquiry and objects of study. Working with other researchers, artists, and designers, I explore how different forms of documentation and analysis can generate fresh insights and understanding.

Recent activities include coordinating a team ethnography of a political festival, crafting “tiny software tools” for ethnographic field notes, and exploring how different groups organise and access collective knowledge. Whether working with knowledge graphs or generative language models, I remain focused on creating conditions for sustained attention and shared discovery.
